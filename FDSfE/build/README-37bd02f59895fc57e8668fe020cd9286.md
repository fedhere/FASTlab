# Class notes for Foundations of Data Science for Everyone. 


Foundations of Data Science for Everyone is an experimental class taught jointly at the University of Delaware and Lincoln University of PA. The target audience are undergraduates in and out of STEM. The class aims at providing a conceptual understanding of AI and Data Science, mastery in the use of computational tools for data managing and handling, and familiarity with common models for the extraction of information from data.

### Approach to class and expectations

The course will be organized in a modular fashion. Each machine learning method will be studied as it is applied to real world problems based on open data and literature examples.

Don't worry about how much you already know, especially do not compare it to what other students know. You may have the wrong perception of your skills, and of the skills of your classmates, and your strengths and the strengths of your background may be less obvious than, say coding or math, but you may have graphical design experience, for example, and be especially helpful in designing good visualizations. Some of you may be better writers and be able to write good captions, others may have more experience coding. All these components make proficiency in this class, and all these components make a good scientist. We, the class assistants and I, are here to help you develop the skills you do not yet have and strengthen the skills you already have.

This class is taught in remote synchronous modality.

## 2. Course Description

This course teaches the basics of data science. Students are not expected to have a STEM background. Some familiarity with computer coding (in python) is helpful, but not required. You will acquire basic computational skills, knowledge of statistical analysis, error analysis, good and ethical practices for handling, processing, and analyzing data programmatically, and communication and visualization skills. Some of the simpler algorithms are explored in detail and implemented from scratch, others are implemented through the use of dedicated python libraries. The course also uses industry-ready tools (e.g. Python libraries, Git, Slack).

### Prerequisites

No prerequisites

### Course Delivery

This is a remote synchronous class taught jointly to cohorts at two institutions. Lectures may be recorded and made available on canvas and occasional absence from in-person lecture is acceptable. Videos recordings will not be curated especially for asynchronous attendance.

Each class includes a lecture, most classes include labs and collaborative coding - always bring an internet connected device with a comfortable keyboard to type on.

Group work is encouraged outside and inside of the lectures.

## 3. Learning Outcomes

By the end of this class, you should be able to formulate an appropriate analysis plan for a research question, select, gather, and prepare data for analysis, and choose and apply machine learning methods to the data.

Students will have a deep understanding of the scientific method and best practices for formulating hypothesis, treating data, interpreting and communicating analysis results.

Students will have a strong grasp of descriptive statistics, AI methodology and modern frameworks for the extraction of information from data.

### Class performance and participation

Each week you will attend two classes, which will be a mix of lectures and hands-on lab work. Attendance in lectures is mandatory and all code developed in class has to be turned in at the end of the lecture in the student's github repo.

### Homework

Weekly assignments will be handed out at the end of the class and will be due strictly before the first class of the following week (no submissions at all can be accepted after that as the homework may be reviewed in class).

Late homework will not be accepted. A single 72-hour exception is allowed throughout the semester, explicitly declare that you are going to use it before the deadline, and do use it wisely. The lowest grade in the first half of the course (before midterm) and the lowest grade in the second half will be disregarded in assigning you a final grade. If you fail to turn in an assignment that will be a 0, and (likely) the lowest grade. This means you will lose the chance to disregard your worst performance.

I encourage you to work in groups of up to 3 people, but as a collaborative project. Individual notebooks must be uploaded to your own github repository for each homework. Different group members should lead different aspects of the work. A statement must be included in the README of the repository explaining each team member's contribution (similar to an acknowledgment of contribution you would find in a Nature letter see, for example, these contributions).

### Midterm

The midterm will be in class. Midterm and Final will include aspects of the work developed in the homework sessions. Failing to actively participate in the homework will result in not being able to get the Midterm and Final done. For the Midterm and the Final, you are responsible for the material in the labs, and the homework.

### Final

The final will be a take home exam but a short (15 min) live session will be scheduled individually with each student. The grade starting point for the final exam is the written final grade. The student will be asked to reproduce a piece of the analysis live with the instructor present and to answer a conceptual question during the oral 1-1 meeting. The outcome of the oral 1-1 meeting can increase the final grade by up to 20 points or decrease it by an unlimited number of points. Midterm and Final will include aspects of the work developed in the homework sessions. Failing to actively participate in the homework will result in not being able to get the Midterm and Final done. For the Midterm and the Final, you are responsible for the material in the labs, and the homework.

### Learning Assessments

Following SMART (Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, Timely) teaching practices, we look at assessment holistically. In particular, this course includes:

- Blend formative and summative assessments to get a full picture of student learning and teaching effectiveness.
- Use student engagement metrics and qualitative feedback to capture insights beyond test scores (participation, continuous feedback on quizzes that assess conceptual understanding and ensure students keep up with the material throughout the semester).
- Leverage technology including Google Forms and interactive coding platforms.
- Incorporate self-reflection and peer observation to gain fresh perspectives on your teaching: assignments grades are focused on reflection on results (via captions and comments) rather than coding and solutions. Collaborative work is encouraged while declaration of contribution allow the students to ensure their participation is effective by self-reflection, the course includes peer review assignments and class activities include pair and share and group discussions.
- Avoid common pitfalls like over-assessing or bias by maintaining fairness and transparency: assignment points are declared ahead and grading is done blindly to the extent that it is feasible.
- Ongoing assessment and points for improvement over time are awarded.
